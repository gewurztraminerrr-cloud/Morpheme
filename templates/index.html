What are the different dictionaries used?

Morpheme supports three distinct word lists/dictionaries that are dynamically spun or configured for each round:

  • NWL (North American Word List): The primary dictionary used for standard tournaments and North American wordplay. It currently contains - words.
  • CSW (Collins Scrabble Words): The authoritative international Scrabble dictionary, containing - words.
  • AW (Added Words): A custom moderator-curated dictionary allowing live, customized word additions for dynamic community-driven solving. It currently has - words. AW strictly only includes custom words that are neither found in NWL nor CSW. If a future update of NWL or CSW incorporates any word currently in the AW list, those words are automatically removed from AW.
  • 16+ List (Supplementary): A specialized collection of words containing 16 or more letters to support solving on larger grids (5x7, 6x8, and Cubes). It currently has - words.

What are the differences between Easy, Medium, and Hard, and why are they set up this way?

Board difficulty in Morpheme is mathematically driven by the Uniqueness Ratio—the proportion of scorable words on a board that belong to the "Unique" dictionary subsets (words found in only one major dictionary, e.g., in NWL but not CSW, or vice versa). Obscure tournament vocabulary is highly unique, whereas highly common words are shared across dictionaries.

By strictly filtering boards based on this ratio and controlling letter frequencies, the system guarantees distinct playing experiences:

Easy Difficulty

Uniqueness Range: 0% – 15% on standard 4x4 boards (up to 34% on 6x8/Cube grids).
Setup & Rationale: Engineered with high-density vowel and common consonant distributions. These boards favor standard, high-frequency vocabulary. Because almost all words are common and shared across lists, they are highly accessible, easy to spot, and perfectly suited for beginners or fast-paced casual play.

Medium Difficulty

Uniqueness Range: 16% – 29% on standard 4x4 boards (35% – 49% on 6x8/Cube grids).
Setup & Rationale: Uses balanced standard letter distributions. These boards offer the ultimate competitive sweet spot—blending highly familiar everyday words with a moderate sprinkling of dictionary-specific or tournament-grade anagrams to push intermediate players.

Hard Difficulty

Uniqueness Range: 30% – 100% on standard 4x4 boards (50%+ on 6x8/Cube grids).
Setup & Rationale: Generated using sparse letter frequencies and rare letter sets (incorporating tougher combinations like Z, X, J, K, etc.). These boards demand extensive specialized vocabulary and advanced anagram-scanning skills, populating the grid with complex roots, affixes, and rare tournament-exclusive words.

What does Morpheme mean?

In linguistics, a morpheme is the smallest grammatical and meaningful unit of a language (such as roots, prefixes, and suffixes like play, un-, or -ful) that cannot be divided into smaller parts. In our game, players act as lexical engineers, linking adjacent letters and using these morphological building blocks to construct high-scoring words!

What do the various highlights on the "All Words" table signify?

Our "All Words" table employs a sophisticated color-coding system to provide at-a-glance insights into the round's results:

  • Lime Green - The Bonus Word. Successfully finding this word awards you an extra point premium equal to its letter length (in addition to its base score).
  • Vibrant Blue - Words you personally discovered during the round, or the words another player found if a username is selected.
  • Golden Yellow - CSW-Only words. These are lexical gems found in the Collins Scrabble Words dictionary but absent from the NWL.
  • Dark Slate - Standard NWL Words that were present on the board.
  • Royal Purple - Added Words. Special words curated by moderators that are currently present on the board.
  • ✓ Checkmark - Indicates words successfully identified by you, a competitor, or multiple players in the room.
How many points do Bonus Words give, and how does length affect the score?

Every board features a single hidden Bonus Word. Uncovering it grants a massive points boost! The score is calculated by taking the word's base game score and adding a Bonus Word Premium equal to the exact letter length of the word.

How does the rating system evaluate my performance and skill?

Morpheme utilizes a refined Proportional Share engine designed to ensure ultimate fairness. Unlike traditional win/loss systems, our engine evaluates your individual contribution relative to the collective strength of the room.

1. The Expected Score
At the conclusion of each round, the server calculates a tailored "Expected Score" for you. This target represents your fair share of the points based on your current rating relative to your opponents. Masters are naturally expected to achieve higher scores, while newcomers have a more accessible performance baseline.

2. Room Stability & Population
The integrity of your rating is bolstered by the number of participants. A larger gathering of players provides a more stable and accurate performance baseline, ensuring that rating adjustments are precise and reflective of your true mastery. More people in the room means more stability in the rating results.

3. Dynamic Gains and Losses
Your final rating change is determined by how significantly you exceed or fall below your Expected Score. Adjustments occur on a precise 0-16 scale, rewarding consistent excellence while maintaining ranking stability through a capped maximum change per round (except during Double and Triple formats, which multiply these changes by two and three respectively, allowing adjustments up to 32 or 48 points).

4. PE (Performance Efficiency)
PE is a real-time metric that represents the ratio of your Actual Score to your Expected Score. A PE of 1.0 means you are playing exactly at your rating's expected level. Exceeding a PE of 1.0 indicates you are outperforming your current rank. Achieving an exceptional, dynamically scaled PE threshold (ranging from 1.4 in 2-player rooms up to 4.0 in rooms of 20+ players) along with a strong raw score (≥40) marks an Exceptional Round, earning you a trophy for that game.

5. Mid-Round, Leaving, and Rejoining Scenarios
To protect game integrity, ratings are adjusted based on participation timing and stability:

  • Joining Mid-Round: If you join a room while a round is actively running, you are flagged as a late joiner. Your rating will not change at the end of that round, nor will you receive any share of the abandonment bounty pool. You will be fully eligible for rating changes starting with the next round.
  • Leaving and Returning: If you started a round from the beginning and temporarily disconnect, refresh, or leave the page, your progress (words and score) is preserved. Rejoining before the round ends fully restores your active status, and you remain fully eligible for rating changes at the end of the round.
  • Abandoning a Round (Leaving Permanently): If you leave during an active round without returning:

    • 24-Hour Rooms: No rating changes or abandonment penalties apply.

    • Standard Rooms: If you leave after finding words or scoring points, your rating will be calculated proportionally at the end of the round using the score you had when you left (meaning you will likely lose rating points up to the round cap). Additionally, your departure adds a flat abandonment bounty of 8 points to a pool, which is distributed equally among the remaining players who started the round. If you return before the round ends, this bounty is reversed.

What are the distinct strategies for the different game types?

Morpheme offers three unique arenas, each demanding a different tactical approach:

  • Accumulative: The classic experience of independent discovery. Every player explores the board at their own pace, receiving full points for every word found, regardless of others' performance.
  • First Come First Serve (FCFS): A high-stakes race where words are a finite resource. The moment a word is claimed, it is removed from the board for all other players. Speed and decisiveness are your primary weapons.
  • Split Points: A strategic blend of discovery and uniqueness. Points for each word are shared among everyone who finds it. While common words yield split rewards, uncovering unique words grants you the full bounty.

What are the different game Formats and what do they do?

Normal (66%): The standard gameplay experience where everything is balanced for classic play.

Checkerboard (12%): Alternates vowels and consonants along rows or columns. This rhythmic layout mirrors the common phonetic construction of words (consonant-vowel-consonant-vowel), occasionally interspersed with adjacent consonants or vowels to mimic natural orthographic patterns.

Equality Freq (4%): Generates balanced, high-word-count boards (200+) using an equalized letter frequency where vowels have a weight of 700, rare consonants (J, Q, X, Z) have a weight of 25, and all other letters have a weight of 413, without using the Word Soup embedding method. By flattening the frequency weights, less-likely letters have a more equal chance of appearing, making word discovery more difficult.

Bounce (2%): Colorful glossy balls bounce around the board, partially obscuring letters temporarily to test your memory and speed as you spot words. This format features three distinct speed levels: 1x (slow, gentle bouncing), 2x (moderate speed), and 3x (extremely fast motion, requiring split-second recognition and recall).

Density (2%): A high-contrast visual format where tiles reflect their "lexical weight." Tiles are shaded in grayscale based on how many words pass through them. Darker tiles indicate high-density letters that are part of many possible words, while lighter tiles have lower connectivity. In dynamic rounds, these shades shift as words are discovered!

[Letter] Mania (2%): Features a high concentration of a single designated letter on the board (e.g., "S" Mania). The target letter occupies 33% or more of all board tiles for common letters, or 20% or more for rare consonants. This letter changes each round, creating a focused board where words containing it are prominent.

Penalty (2%): Exercise caution! Any sequence found on the board that is NOT in the dictionary results in a 3-point deduction.

Either/Or (2%): Exactly one tile on the board contains two possible letters (e.g., C/H). Words utilizing either of these letters receive a +3 point bonus. When playing, the game determines which letter you intended based on where you touch the tile or the word you type.

Bonus Letter (2%): One letter on the board is highlighted in a vibrant, persisting color. Words containing this letter grant +3 extra points.

Valued Letters (2%): Each letter has a custom numerical point value (e.g., Q=10, E=1). Word scores are the sum of their letter values.

Rotation (2%): A purely visual challenge! Every 4 seconds, the letters on the board rotate randomly by 90, 180, or 270 degrees, testing your spatial awareness and ability to recognize words from different angles!

Double (1%): An electrifying multiplier format! All rating gains and losses at the end of the round are multiplied by two. THIS, AND TRIPLE, IS THE ONLY TIME A RATING CHANGE CAN EXTEND BEYOND THE LIMITS OF -16 AND +16. The highest and lowest rating change possible is +32 and -32.

Triple (1%): The ultimate high-stakes format! All rating gains and losses at the end of the round are multiplied by three. THIS, AND DOUBLE, IS THE ONLY TIME A RATING CHANGE CAN EXTEND BEYOND THE LIMITS OF -16 AND +16. The highest and lowest rating change possible is +48 and -48.

What determines the difficulty level of a board?

Board difficulty is scientifically determined by the ratio of obscure or complex words to common vocabulary. If over 75% to 80% of the possible words are statistically difficult to uncover, the board is classified as Difficult. Conversely, if 80% to 90% of the words are common and easily found, the board is considered Easy.

Why does the system generate unique parameters for each round?

Every round is a fresh challenge governed by the Spinner Set. These randomly generated parameters—including word density, dictionary selection, and board format—ensure that no two games are ever alike. This prevents memorization and maintains a fair, unpredictable, and ever-evolving competitive landscape.

Spinner Word Count Range Distribution:

  • 9% chance of 50 - 100 words (Snug, intense boards)
  • 30% chance of 100 - 200 words (Balanced challenge)
  • 30% chance of 200 - 300 words (High density exploration)
  • 30% chance of 300 - 400 words (Massive, word-rich arenas)
  • 1% chance of 500+ words (Colossal word arenas)
  • 📝 Note: For CSW + AW and NWL + AW rounds, the distribution shifts to: 33% (300-400 words), 33% (400-500 words), and 34% (500+ words) to comfortably fit the combined vocabulary.

What is the "Combo Checker" in the Tools tab?

ELI5 (Explain Like I'm Five): Imagine the Combo Checker is like a word wizard that shows you how to change one word into another on the board! If you have one word and want to turn it into a new one, the wizard shows you all the tiny steps to get there. This includes insertions (adding a new letter), removals (taking a letter away), and switches (changing one letter into a different one). It's like a map that shows you the fastest way to travel from word to word!

How are boards generated?

Boards are generated using a multi-stage background process to ensure a fair, competitive, and lag-free experience:

  • 1. Parameter Roll: The system rolls for dictionary (NWL/CSW), difficulty, target word count range, and board format based on specific weighted probabilities.
  • 2. The 'Word Soup' Pool: It loads the selected dictionary and creates a pool of valid words between 5 and 10 letters long, strictly filtering out any words ending in 'ING' or 'INGS' to let those emerge naturally.
  • 3. Embedding the Soup: The generator attempts to snake as many words from the filtered pool (~45 words) as possible into the grid, filling empty spaces to create a rich environment.
  • 4. Bonus Word Guarantee: It selects a specific Bonus Word matching the length determined by the parameters (or your custom settings in Solo mode). It then "stamps" this word onto the board, overwriting letters along a random path to guarantee its presence.
  • 5. Smart Random Fill: Any remaining empty cells are filled with random letters based on optimized English frequency distributions. In Medium and Hard modes, the system actively blocks letter placements that would accidentally form 'I-N-G' sequences.
  • 6. Post-Solve Verification: Because random filling creates accidental extra words, the server runs an authoritative solver to count every valid word on the board. If the count falls outside the target range (meaning we broke too many words or created too many), it discards the board and retries up to 5 times.

To ensure instant game starts, this entire process is pre-calculated in the background while you are still playing the previous round!

Why are the 2D boards that are not 4x4 not a perfect square?

That is to accommodate the shape of the screen of the users who are using their mobile devices to play Morpheme!

Why is the cube size adjustable when there are no playable cubes?
That would be because you are playing on your mobile device! Mobile devices are not capable of rendering the 3D cube board — but desktops and laptops can. The cube size setting is still shown in your preferences so that it is ready and waiting the moment you switch to a larger device.
The parameters shown on the Spinner Set did not match the parameters of the actual round. Why?

This is a known and intentional safety mechanism. Here is exactly what happens behind the scenes:

  1. Pre-generation sanitization. Before the board generator even begins, the server inspects the spun parameters and checks whether they are mathematically compatible with each other. For example, a very high Minimum Word Length combined with a very small board dimension may make it geometrically impossible to place enough qualifying words. In these cases, one or more parameters are quietly adjusted to a safer value before generation starts.
  2. Search timeout and retries. The board generator attempts to find a valid board that satisfies all of the spun constraints simultaneously — density, word count, word length range, format rules, and more. It is given a strict time budget to do this. If it cannot find a qualifying board within that window after multiple retries, it does not stall the game; instead, it progressively relaxes the hardest constraints (such as minimum word count or minimum word length) until a valid board is found.
  3. Emergency clean-slate fallback. If relaxation still fails, the generator falls back to a clean-slate set of parameters — for instance, forcing the Density format on grids that require very long minimum-length words, because Density is the format most likely to produce a fully satisfying board under tight constraints. The round proceeds normally; only the displayed parameters change to reflect what was actually used.

In short: the Spinner Set shows what was spun, but the round reflects what was achievable. The system always prioritizes getting a high-quality, playable board into your hands over rigidly honoring every spun value when doing so would be impossible or take too long.

Why does dragging from the top and pressing the back arrow at the bottom not exit full screen on mobile?

When playing Morpheme in PWA/standalone mode, the app uses immersive full screen to maximize space for the game board. Note that the Android system prompt to "drag from the top and touch the back button" (or pressing the back arrow at the bottom) will not exit full screen because the app is configured to remain immersive during play. To minimize or close the app, swipe up from the bottom edge of your screen to reveal your device's home indicator/bar, then swipe up again to return to your home screen or view the app switcher.
